Analysts put task mining on a 65–90% growth curve through 2026, driven almost entirely by AI-agent design use cases. The argument in five steps

  • The category is real and it's accelerating. Everest and Gartner both frame 2026 growth as AI-agent-driven, not workforce-productivity-driven — the market is re-platforming around "who feeds AI agents context," not "who watches desktops."
  • Celonis is under-positioned, not under-capable. Buyers and even Celonis's own field team default to seeing Celonis as a Process Intelligence vendor first — the deck's own customer-POV section says this directly, and it shows up as a repeated loss pattern.
  • Pyze is a strong leverage for wining Task mining deals. Two named competitive displacements — Merck (vs. Mimica) and Unilever (vs. Soroco) — exist today won by Pyze. Pyze+Celonis offeres broader use cases, stronger competitive advantage and trusted market positioning.  Pyze can still work on updating CCM messaging and recent Celonis roadmap capabilities and strengths.  
  • Pricing is not where Celonis is structurally losing. Only 4 of 14 lost deals cite pricing; 9 cite feature depth. Competitive offers attactive and bundled commerical positioning that celonis should work on to offer its customers. 

"In an AI-first landscape, this level of desktop visibility is indispensable. Combining granular task data with broader process mining creates the complete foundation for AI-driven process redesign, agentic recommendations, and end-to-end automation."   - Bastian Nominacher 

 
The fight to win is not "Celonis vs. four task-mining point solutions on features" — it's "Celonis vs. four point solutions on who owns the context layer AI agents will run on."

Mimica's pricing-model reversal — resolved

In 2024, Mimica piloted a prepaid AI-credit/consumption-based pricing model across its suite. The model triggered client backlash over transparency — customers could not predict or understand how many credits were deducted per action. Deal qualification — green lights vs. red flags

Green light for Celonis

  • Needs a platform to build automations — Celonis identifies RPA/agent opportunities and generates executable automation scripts natively.
  • Needs speed and enterprise scale — Celonis zero-copy, patented PQL, 2,500+ users/project, 30,000+/instance, 30B+ events/day.
  • Needs end-to-end process ROI (discovery through execution) in a single platform.
  • Needs complex multi-system integration — 100+ native connectors vs. Mimica's desktop-only capture.

Red flag / proceed cautiously

  • Buyer explicitly needs macOS deployment today — Mimica is the only vendor of the four that supports it now (Celonis macOS support is roadmapped, no committed GA date stated).
  • Fast pilot on a constrained budget — Mimica's low-setup, low-cost POC (well under Celonis's typical enterprise commitment) is genuinely attractive for "test the waters" pilots.

Mimica's blind spots vs. Celonis's advantage

The architectural data disconnect

Mimica's limitation: confined to the desktop — relies on a recorder observing UI clicks/keystrokes, no native backend event-log extraction at the database level. Celonis: Process Intelligence Graph ingests actual database transactions via 100+ native connectors, combining backend truth with desktop task data. Why it matters: seeing a click doesn't show the systemic bottleneck in the database — Celonis shows the financial impact of that click across ERPs and supply chain.

The organizational context void

Mimica's task maps show how one user completes a task, not organizational relationships, role-level handoffs, or how one invoice relates to ten purchase orders. Celonis's OCPM reconstructs the end-to-end structural lifecycle across multi-dimensional workflows into a centralized repository.

The temporal (ROI) gap

Mimica operates in fixed "recording windows" (e.g. two weeks) — the map stops updating once the project ends, with no continuous historical comparison. Celonis runs continuous month-over-month comparisons to prove automation ROI over time.

The structural simulation ceiling

Mimica can estimate the ROI of automating a specific task but can't apply structural optimization levers (eliminating/consolidating entire steps) across the system landscape. Celonis tests the impact of removing or redesigning structural steps before executing them globally.
